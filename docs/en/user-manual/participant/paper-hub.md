# Paper Hub

In the Paper Hub you write your delegation's papers, submit them to the team and read the feedback on them. Supervisors see the papers of the delegations they supervise there.

## Overview {#overview}

You reach the Paper Hub through the **Paper Hub** card in the **Quick Links** on your [dashboard](./dashboard#preparation). The card appears as soon as the team opens paper submission.

At the top you choose between the views that apply to you:

- **My Papers:** your own papers and the buttons for a new one. Only for members of a delegation.
- **Supervisor View:** the papers of the delegations you supervise. Only for supervisors (see [For supervisors](#supervisors)).
- **Conference Papers:** the submitted papers you may read, by committee and agenda item (see [Who sees what](#who-sees-what)).

## Paper types {#paper-types}

Which papers you can write depends on your delegation's role:

- **Position paper:** describes the stance of your state or non-state actor on an agenda item. Every delegation writes position papers.
- **Working paper:** a proposal for a resolution that you would like the committee to adopt. Delegations representing a state write working papers.
- **Introduction paper:** introduces your non-state actor, its topics and its stance on the United Nations. It belongs to no agenda item. Only non-state actors write one.

If your delegation represents a state, you write only on the agenda items of your own committee. A non-state actor sits in no committee and chooses from every agenda item of the conference.

As a single participant you do not write papers.

## When you can write {#when-you-can-write}

Two things must be true:

- **The roles have been released.** Before that, DELEGATOR refuses to save: “Papers can be written once your delegation's role has been released.”
- **Paper submission is open.** The team switches it on and off. While it is closed, the card disappears from the dashboard and every save is refused: “Paper cannot be submitted because the paper mailbox is closed.” This applies to drafts and revisions too.

:::note Deadlines
The team decides by when papers should be submitted and tells you. DELEGATOR itself knows no deadline, only the switch for submission.
:::

## Writing a paper {#writing-a-paper}

1. Open **My Papers** and, under **Submit paper**, click **Create** for the paper type you want.
2. Under **Paper details** you see your **Delegation** and your **Committee**. Choose the **Agenda item**. An introduction paper has none.
3. Write the text in the editor. Below the editor, DELEGATOR counts words, characters and paragraphs and estimates the reading time.
4. Click **Save draft** to continue later, or **Submit paper** to hand it to the team. You then return to the overview.

Without an agenda item or without text, DELEGATOR saves nothing and tells you what is missing.

:::tip Nothing gets lost
While you write a new paper, your browser keeps the text every second. If you close the tab by accident, DELEGATOR asks the next time under **Unsaved Draft Found** whether you want to **Restore Draft** or **Start Fresh**. This only works in the same browser and for seven days.
:::

### Working papers in the resolution editor {#working-papers}

You write a working paper in the resolution editor. It sets the resolution's header itself: the conference, the committee, the agenda item and your delegation. Below it you add **Preamble Clauses** and **Operative Clauses**, operative clauses with sub-clauses too. **Phrase Lookup** helps you find suitable opening phrases. You may ignore the **Unknown opening phrase** warning when your wording follows the style of UN resolutions. **Show Preview** shows the resolution as it will look in print.

## Draft or submit {#drafts-and-submitting}

A **draft** is yours alone. Nobody else in your delegation sees it, and the team does not review it. The team only reads a paper once it is submitted.

This is how you continue working on an existing paper:

1. Open it in **My Papers** with **Open**.
2. Change the text. As long as something is unsaved, a warning sign shows **Changes not saved!**
3. For a draft, click **Save draft** or **Submit paper**. For a submitted paper the button is **Resubmit Paper**. It only becomes active once you have changed something.

Every save creates a new version. Earlier versions are kept.

:::warning Once submitted, never a draft again
You cannot turn a submitted paper back into a draft. You can only revise it with a resubmission.
:::

:::caution No browser backup when revising
The automatic backup in your browser only exists for new papers. When you change an existing paper, save before you leave the page.
:::

## After submitting {#after-submitting}

Your paper always has a status:

| Status                  | Meaning                                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Draft**               | Only you see it.                                                |
| **Submitted**           | It waits for the team's first feedback.                         |
| **Revisions requested** | The team asks you to change something.                          |
| **Revised**             | You resubmitted it after feedback. It waits for the team again. |
| **Accepted**            | The team has accepted the paper.                                |

Only the team sets **Accepted** and **Revisions requested**, with a review. Every review sets one of these two.

This is how you revise a paper after feedback:

1. Read the feedback under **History** on the paper's page. If the team quotes a passage, the **Find in paper** button next to the quote jumps to that passage in your text.
2. Change the text and click **Resubmit Paper**.
3. The paper is then **Revised** and waits for the next review.

You can still resubmit an accepted paper. It is then **Revised** again until the team reviews it once more.

:::note An email for every review
Every review comes with the email **New feedback on “…”**, showing the new status and a **View feedback** button. The team decides when it is sent. If you reply to the email, your reply reaches the person who wrote the review.
:::

## Versions and history {#history}

**History** lists every saved version and every review, newest first. For each version you see how much was added and removed. For each review you see who wrote it, the status before and after, and the comments.

To compare two versions, click **Compare with another version** on one version, then on a second one. A window shows the differences.

## Who sees what {#who-sees-what}

- **You** see all your papers with every version and all feedback, drafts included.
- **Your delegation** reads your submitted papers under **Conference Papers**, in read-only mode and only in their latest version. The other members see neither drafts nor feedback.
- **Your supervisors** read your delegation's submitted papers (see below).
- **The team** reads every paper of the conference and reviews the submitted ones.

Under **Conference Papers** the papers are arranged by committee: rows are delegations, columns are agenda items. Each paper is an icon that opens it. Introduction papers have a section of their own. **Search by delegation name...** filters the list.

## For supervisors {#supervisors}

As a supervisor you read the submitted papers of every delegation with someone in it whom you supervise. You do not see drafts. You cannot write papers yourself.

At the top, the **Supervisor View** counts how many papers there are and how many of them are **Submitted**, **Revisions requested** and **Accepted**. Below, the papers are listed by delegation, with type, status, agenda item, **Author** and submission date. This view appears as soon as you supervise someone in a delegation.

A click opens the paper in read-only mode (**You are viewing this paper as a supervisor (read-only access)**). You see the submitted versions under **History**. Only the person who wrote the paper sees the team's feedback.

## Exporting {#export}

On a paper's page, **Export PDF** downloads it. **Export Typst** downloads the source for the Typst typesetting program. A working paper is set as a resolution with its header, every other paper as a text document with a note that it was written as part of a simulation. The export takes the text currently in the editor, even if it is not saved yet.

The buttons are there for you as the author and for supervisors. The read-only view under **Conference Papers** has no export.

## Deleting a paper {#deleting}

You can delete a draft, but not a submitted paper.

1. Open the draft and expand the **Danger Zone** at the very bottom.
2. Type the title shown, such as “GA: Climate change - Germany”.
3. Click **Delete Paper Permanently**.

:::danger Permanent
The paper disappears with all its versions. This cannot be undone.
:::
